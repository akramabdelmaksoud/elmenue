How they price, and what to copy

Foodics anchors the top

About 2,850–3,960 EGP a month for the cloud POS, with extras on top. Your Operations tier at 850–1,000 EGP is roughly a quarter to a third of that. Say it as a saving: “about 25,000–35,000 EGP less per year”. Do not claim to replace its POS or tax features.

Zlenu is your closest rival

It does what you do beside the cashier: QR menu, table ordering, captain, kitchen, loyalty, AI menu scan. It has a free plan, 14-day trials, discounts of 11% and 22% for longer billing, and gates plans by limits, not features. Your Basic tier at 350 EGP must beat a free plan limited to 20 dishes and 5 tables.

Everyone else sells to chains

Dgtera and VeraKit360 hide prices and sell demos to larger operators. Besalla and Ordable sell branded apps and websites for delivery. A small independent café is least served, and that is where Basic and Operations fit.

What is changing in the market

New entrants

Qatar’s DineNORDER announced its Egypt launch in August 2026 with ordering, POS, reservations and inventory. Wamda. Foreign and Saudi vendors are moving in; being local, Arabic first and fast to support is your defence.

Payments are bundled

Foodics partnered with Paymob for card terminals in Egypt, GrubTech enrols its venues with Paymob automatically, and Simple Touch added Fawry. Foodics. Leaders earn from payments, not only software. Plan a gateway partnership.

E-receipts

The tax authority rolls out electronic receipts by named taxpayer, in waves, and sources disagree on dates. A venue can check whether its registration number is on the authority’s e-receipt inquiry page. Guide. Check each prospect before you promise anything.

Pricing & the numbers

Monthly leads. The one-time licence is the answer for owners who refuse a subscription. Quotes expire in 7 to 14 days because the pound moves.

Add-ons

Printer and cash drawer setup 1,500–3,000 EGP. Card payments 3,000–8,000 EGP once built. Custom work 500–1,000 EGP an hour. QR table stands 100–250 EGP each. Hardware at cost plus 10–15%.

Pricing rules

How to read it, as a business

Setup fees fund growth

A blended 3,500 setup fee covers the 1,500 cash cost of each new venue and part of your time. That is why growth does not drain your cash in year one.

Churn is the lever

Monthly churn of 3% means a venue stays 33 months. At 6% it stays 17 and lifetime value halves. The monthly owner report, WhatsApp support and a loyalty feature the owner depends on keep churn low.

Your time is the real cost

Cash margin looks like 84%. Count 1.5 support hours a month and it is about 37%. Training videos, a printed card and a clean handover are what make the second hundred venues cheaper than the first ten.

Chapter eight

Zero-downtime rollout

Agile development with DevOps habits, introduced by pilot and then in phases, with payments run in parallel. A venue never depends on Table Tab until it has proven itself beside the old process.

Week 0

Prepare offline

Import the menu, photos, tables and staff into a private copy. The owner checks every price. Guests see nothing.

Week 1

QR-only trial

One QR on one or two tables, ordering switched off. If anything fails, remove the stand and service is unchanged.

Week 2

Shadow mode

Guests order on the phone while staff also run the old way. Compare totals at close; every difference becomes a bug.

Week 3

Quiet hours live

Mornings and weekday afternoons run on Table Tab. The old process waits on the counter.

Week 4

Payments in parallel

Cash and card are recorded in both systems for five to seven days until daily totals match to the pound.

Week 5 onward

Peak hours

Only after a quiet week with zero blocking bugs and an experienced person on every shift. Paper chits stay in a drawer for 30 days.

Day 30

Retire the old process

Only when the owner signs the handover record.

Technical safety net

Separate production, staging and preview environments. Deploys only outside service hours, one change at a time, each tagged so rollback takes under five minutes. New modules ship switched off and are enabled per venue. Every data change must tolerate old data. Nightly off-site backups with a weekly restore test. Automated tests for billing, order lock, loyalty and tenant isolation run before every release. Uptime and error alerts go to your phone.

First sprint: hardening

Rotate the Supabase key and every token ever shared in chat. Change the live owner password and captain PIN. Rate-limit logins. Confirm every route checks the venue. Move off the Vercel free plan, which is non-commercial, before the first paid venue. Add SMS verification before storing real customer phone numbers. Have a lawyer review the contract and privacy clause.

Data checklist

Menu, prices, photos, tables and zones, staff, tax and service charge, hours. Import into a private copy. Owner signs a printed price sheet. Print and test every QR on two phone types. Change all default logins.

Training

Owner 60 minutes. Waiters a 20-minute drill. Kitchen 15 minutes. Everyone gets a printed Arabic card for when the screen or internet fails.

Never during service

Changing prices, taxes or table numbers. Deploying a new version. Resetting statistics. Moving a venue to another tier without telling the staff.

Handover record

What was installed and which tier. Who holds which login. Backup schedule and last restore test. Support hours and WhatsApp number. Known limits, such as no ETA and no offline mode. The date the old process may be retired. Signed by the owner and by you.

Your three proofs

Live demo on their phone. A free one-table QR trial for a week. Your written promise that the old process stays until they sign.

The 10-minute demo, in order

Minutes 0–1

Their phone scans a QR

Open the demo, scan, land on the Home screen with their name and colours. They see themselves first.

Minutes 1–3

Order and watch the kitchen

Order two drinks from the phone. Show the kitchen screen ringing. Press the water button and show the alert with the table number.

Minutes 3–5

The waiter and the bill

Accept the order, mark it ready, split the bill, pay in cash. Show the receipt.

Minutes 5–8

The owner’s screen

Open Analytics: today’s revenue, best sellers, rush hours. Say: “this is what you do not know today.”

Minutes 8–10

Switch tier and close

Use the tier switcher to show Basic, Operations and VIP. Ask: “which of these hurts most today?”

Questions to ask the owner

  • How many tables, how many waiters per shift?
  • When is your rush, and what goes wrong in it?
  • What do you use today: paper, a POS, another QR menu?
  • Are you already registered for e-receipts?
  • Who owns the decision: you, a partner, a manager?
  • What do you pay monthly for software today?
  • How often do prices change, and what does reprinting cost?
  • Do you know your best-selling item and your profit this week?
  • Do guests ask for water, napkins or the bill repeatedly?
  • Do you have more than one branch or plan to?

Negotiation rules

Give

A longer free trial, 20% off yearly, 10% off quarterly, a discount for being a pilot, the setup fee waived for a referral.

Trade

Every discount is traded for something: a testimonial, photos, a referral, a signed date. Never a bare price cut.

Hold

The monthly floor of 350 EGP, the one-time floor of 15,000 EGP, and the written handover before the old process is retired.

Technical debt and risks

  • Whole venue is one JSON document saved with compare-and-swap. Busy venues will see more save retries as order volume grows.
  • Guests and staff poll the server every 2.5 seconds on Vercel. Cost and delay grow with venues; plan a real-time server for larger tenants.
  • Large single-file pages (customer, admin, captain) with inline script. Fast to build, harder to maintain and test.
  • .old_server.js.bak and .local.err are tracked in git.
  • Docs (SPEC_COVERAGE, PROJECT_GUIDE) no longer match the code: shifts, feedback, coupons and limits are not described.
  • The eight features live on the release/next branch; merge to main only after the browser and real-device checks.
  • Loyalty identifies people by phone number only, with no verification.
  • Copilot is keyword-based; real answers depend on an optional API key and its cost.
  • Secrets were shared in chats earlier (your own security to-do): rotate them.
